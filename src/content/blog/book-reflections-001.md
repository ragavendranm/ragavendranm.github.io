I love serendipitous discoveries when browsing book shelves in libraries. I don’t bother to read reviews or wonder if a book will be worth my time. I just pick a book on a whim and give it a shot. If the book engages me, I read it in its entirety. Otherwise, I simply return it.

During my recent rummaging around in the local library, I stumbled upon this book. Less than 100 pages long. The size of your average paperback. The back cover which said *“… takes the reader from the start to finish of any long-form project..”*. Well, that was enough for me. I picked it up, and completed it.

First off, was it any good? Given the short read time (about an hour I think), and some interesting ideas in it, I would say it was fine. If the book was any longer, I would have probably not gone through with it. Which begs the question - did this have to be a book in this day and age? This was perhaps good enough to be a long-form read on the web? But I did enjoy the fact that I could hold this book physically in my hands and read through it without any distractions. So no complaints.

**So, what are my key takeaways?**

- **Start before you are ready. Don’t prepare. Begin.** Really good advice, and upon reflection, I have kind of fallen behind or procrastinated whenever I did not do this.
- **Be on a Research Diet. Let the Unconscious do its work.** Personally, I learned this the hard way in grad school. For one of my projects, I remember going into a research rabbit hole, with very little to show for when it was time for the final presentation. The author does say there is a time and place for deep research down the line, but certainly not at the beginning. Sound advice for someone like me.
- **The creative art is primitive.** Babies are born in blood and chaos. The hospital room may be spotless and sterile, but birth itself will always take place amid chaos, pain, and blood. First time I have ever seen anyone link art to child birth, but this passage is evocative and I think will stick in my brain for a while.
- **Swing for the seats.** If you want to do great stuff, go big. You can always dial it down later if you want, but start big.
- **Stay Stupid.** Again, unconventional, until you look at the cited examples. Charles Lindbergh, Steve Jobs, Winston Churchill. Be clueless enough to have no idea how difficult your enterprise is going to be, but be cocky enough to believe you can pull it off anyway. I am going to have a hard time following this advice! My brain is simply not wired this way unfortunately.
- **Passion. A child plays with it all day long.** All I need to do is watch my kid to realize the truth in this advice.
- **Act, then reflect on ideas, but never at the same time.** For the first draft, go heavy on action, but light on reflection. Solid advice for someone like me who gets all hung up on issues with my first draft :-)
- **Rewriting is inevitable for good writing.** Sigh. True, but what I dread the most.
- **Ship it.** Again, been a big blocker for me. I have at least 3-4 drafts lying around waiting to find the light of the day. Reading this book gave me enough motivation to start off with something small (like this very article) before I lost the courage to hit the publish button again.

I will remember this book for serving as a motivation to publishing my first article in almost seven years!
