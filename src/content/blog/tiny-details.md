I visited India back in 2024, and for a bunch of reasons, decided to switch to Singapore Airlines.  The flight journey and the in-flight service itself were not particularly remarkable.  However, two things on those flights left a lasting impression in my mind that I was motivated to write about them even after 12+ months: (1) Their in-flight safety video and (2) The UX of the entertainment system. 

## Creativity with In-Flight Safety

Let’s talk about the video first.  It’s best if you can watch the video for yourself first (runtime of 5:49) before diving into the details:

Now that you have watched it, let’s revisit some of the creative delights within this production:

- [0:22](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=22): We see “Singapore Girl" beginning to descend the stairs at the Boat Quay.  In the next cut, she is descending down the stairs to the Intan Pernakan Home Museum.  Just a wonderful, seamless transition (more of the same at [1:44](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=104), [3:04](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=185), and [3:54](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=234)).
- [0:37](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=37): Even the props are intentional.  The antique chair the guest is seated on actually has reclining capability, syncing perfectly with the “put the back of your seat upright” message.
- [1:49](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=109): Ok, this entire stretch was genius.  The masks serve two purposes: first, as oxygen masks for the safety instructions, and second, as protective gear for the father and child to spray paint.  The icing on the cake is the reveal that they were painting an aircraft, which is then used to display the emergency exits on the aircraft! 
- [2:36](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=156): I loved how they accounted for “can reach / can’t reach the seat in front of you” in one stroke.
- [3:10](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=190): Another brilliant segment that artistically blends evacuation procedures with water park activities!  Leaving shoes and belongings behind, putting on a life vest, stepping into a flotation device - normal water park activities, packaged artistically as life-saving evacuation procedures!
- [4:05](https://youtu.be/25brQSPMORg?t=245): A meta moment where the audience in the theatre is watching the very video that unfolds 2 seconds later!
- [The whole video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dOpwFr5-iEw): Excellent videography, lighting, background music, and the creative use of each location for the safety instructions.  Certainly the only time I have ever looked forward to viewing an in-flight safety video in all my years of flying.

From my perspective, that’s a lot of creativity and artistry packed into a five-minute video.  They managed to take a dry information video and infuse it with glimpses of Singapore’s main attractions in a very elegant fashion. 

## Dark Mode Shines

A lot of people I know take advantage of long-haul flights to catch up on movies or TV shows.  Not me.  I typically use the majority of a 21+ hour journey to catch up on my sleep.  I usually end up using the in-flight entertainment system to check flight stats (location, outside temperature, altitude, speed) or to read the latest news headlines, followed by about 2 hours of actual screen time when I am bored with everything else.  I appreciate the system for the utility it brings to me as a traveler while on such a long flight.

But you know what I hate about these systems?  They have bright screens that are right in front of your face at all times during the flight.  While you can dial down the brightness or turn them off, they have a tendency to turn on when the cabin is dark, which really hurts the eyes.  Several times, I have wished for the ability to unplug the screen as soon as the safety procedure demonstrations are out of the way, and only plug it back in for descent. 

![Dark Mode](/images/blog/tiny-details/dark-mode.jpeg)

So you can imagine my joy when I finally encountered an in-flight entertainment UX with dark mode!  I admit that the navigation experience was not perfect - for instance, there was no physical button to turn off the screen, and it took me a while to figure out how to do it within the software.  But whenever the screen turned on in a dark cabin, whether by accident or intentionally by the cabin crew, there was no bright white light to hurt my eyes - just the gentleness of the dark mode.  It’s a rather mundane UX concept in this day and age, but one that I did not truly appreciate until this flight journey! 

## The Bottom Line

Tiny details like these add up, creating strong brand recognition.  Ironically, I faced flight delays both on onward and return flights.  But I don’t remember those delays anymore; delays happen.  Instead, what has stuck to my mind is the dark mode on the screen, and that creative safety video! 

It goes to show that creativity and attention to detail matter in every aspect of a customer’s experience.  Even when the "big" things (like on-time schedule) go wrong, the small things can win the day.

*P.S: Singapore Airlines now has a newer in-flight safety video.  Follows the same idea and aesthetics with some tweaks (including some cartoon-y illustrations).  I am still a fan of the original, though.*
